This guide explains how to choose blinds for a living room, window by window.

What a living room needs from a blind

A bedroom often needs darkness for sleep. A living room is different: it is usually a balance between daylight, comfort and privacy, and it sees very different light through the day. When people first message us, the problem can sound like this: "Living room gets very hot in the afternoon." or "I want privacy but don't want to lose all my daylight."

Two things to look at first:

  • Which way the window faces. In a west-facing living room with strong afternoon sun, the fabric you choose matters more than anywhere else in the house.
  • The kind of opening. A standard window, a sliding door to the balcony and a full-height glass wall each suit a different blind.

Roller blinds: sunscreen or dim-out

For most living-room windows we suggest a roller blind in one of two fabrics:

  • Sunscreen: an open-weave fabric that filters strong sunlight while keeping some view through the window. It gives daytime privacy and keeps the room feeling open and bright. On our own room guide, it is the fabric we pair with living rooms.
  • Dim-out: sits between sunscreen and blackout. It cuts strong daylight and gives more privacy, while the room keeps some natural brightness. A practical choice for a living room that feels uncomfortable in direct sunlight.

One thing to know about sunscreen fabric: how much you can see through it depends on how open the weave is, its colour and the lighting. At night, when the room is brighter than outside, it gives less privacy than in the day.

Blackout is an opaque fabric that greatly reduces the light coming through. It suits bedrooms, children's rooms and TV rooms better.

Sliding doors and wide glass: VertiSheer

Many living rooms open onto a balcony or garden through a sliding door. A roller blind has to be raised before you step outside; a vertical blind does not. Our VertiSheer blinds are made for this kind of opening: sliding glass doors, balcony doors, full-height windows, wide living-room windows and large glass walls.

  • Open the vanes for soft daylight and the view.
  • Turn them part-way for privacy while the daylight stays: the setting most condo living rooms sit at.
  • Draw the whole blind aside when you want the door fully open.

The soft fabric vanes look closer to a curtain than to a hard blind, which suits a living room where you want warmth without heavy curtains.

Venetian blinds: angle the light

Instead of blocking the window, Venetian blinds let you redirect the sunlight by tilting the slats, so the room stays bright and you can still see outside.

  • Timber or faux-timber: wider slats and a warmer look, suited to living rooms and studies.
  • Aluminium: slimmer slats with a cleaner, more modern look, and easy to wipe clean.

For a kitchen or another humid spot, we usually recommend aluminium instead of wood.

Mixing blinds in one living room

You do not have to pick one type for every window. A living room can have a roller blind on a standard window, VertiSheer on the sliding door and Venetian blinds on a side window. The photo at the top of this guide shows exactly that mix.

Measure the actual window

The difference between a blind that looks built into the window and one that looks slightly awkward is usually the measurement. We measure the actual window on site and check whether an inside mount (inside the window recess) or an outside mount (above and past the opening) works better. An inside mount gives a neat, built-in look when the recess is deep enough. An outside mount covers beyond the window opening, which helps when you want fewer light gaps at the sides.

If you have young children

Looped blind cords and chains are a strangulation risk for babies and young children. The Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents (RoSPA) advises installing any safety devices that come with a new blind, fixing cord cleats at least 1.5 metres from the floor, keeping a child's cot or bed away from the window, and considering a cordless model. A motorised blind also has no chain to pull: a motor raises and lowers it.
